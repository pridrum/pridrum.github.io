## Course Overview

**Institution:** Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ), Rio de Janeiro, Brazil  
**Role:** Project Coordinator & Middle School Teacher  
**Years:** 2006–2009 · 90+ students per year · Ages 11–16

---

## Program Description

This was a project-based learning model school integrating **engineering, design, and
technology** for middle school students. The curriculum centered on boat design and
construction — students learned drawing, geometry, algebra, planning, and hands-on
fabrication using power and woodworking tools.

As curriculum coordinator, I:
- Managed an interdisciplinary team of **10–15 undergraduate interns**
- Developed and taught experiential learning methodologies integrating engineering
  principles with creative design
- Organized 4 major public events per year, each serving **2,000+ community members**
  and employing 120+ student workers and technicians
- Led logistics, community partnerships, local government liaisons, and activity planning

---

## Teaching Philosophy Roots

This was my first real teaching role — and it shaped everything that followed.

The students came from difficult circumstances: poverty, family instability, gang-affected
neighborhoods. I learned quickly that content delivery was the easy part. The harder and
more important work was **active listening**, meeting students where they were, and creating
an environment where they felt they belonged before they could learn.

Some of those students went on to become professors and teachers themselves. Years later,
a few reached out to say I had influenced their path. That is the moment that confirmed
teaching was not just something I do — it is who I am.

This program also produced a nationally recognized achievement: our team at **Polo Náutico
UFRJ** designed and built a **solar-powered boat** that competed internationally and gained
national media attention in Brazil and the Netherlands.
