## Course Overview

**Course:** IE 4516 — Quality Assurance (Undergraduate)
**Role:** Graduate Teaching Assistant, and lead on the redesign of the course teaching materials
**Institution:** Northeastern University, Dept. of Mechanical & Industrial Engineering
**Term:** Fall 2023 (Prof. Hugh McManus, ~20 students)

Quality Assurance covers statistical process control, design of experiments, measurement
systems, sampling, and the management systems that surround them. It is a course where
students can pass every calculation and still not understand what the numbers are for.

---

## Course development

This is the course where I moved from delivering material to building it.

I took the lead on a **complete redesign of the course teaching materials**, restructuring
them around **experimental, simulation-based, and project-based learning**. Rather than
present quality methods and then ask students to practise them, the redesigned materials put
students inside a process first — running it, measuring it, watching it drift — so that the
statistical tools arrive as answers to a problem they have already felt.

What that meant in practice:

- **Replacing lecture content with activities students work through themselves.** The
  material students previously received passively became something they generated.
- **Simulation and hands-on experiments** as the entry point to control charts and process
  variation, so the distinction between common-cause and special-cause variation is observed
  rather than defined.
- **Project-based assessment**, so the course ends in something students have built and can
  explain, rather than a set of exercises.

I then **taught several of the resulting modules myself**, which is what turned the redesign
from a document into evidence — I saw where the new materials worked and where they needed
another pass.

---

## Why this shaped how I teach

Designing a course and teaching it are the same job. You cannot run an active classroom on
materials built for a passive one — the materials either create the space for students to
work, or they quietly close it. Every course I have designed since, including
[OR 6205](/teachings/or6205-deterministic-or/), started from that lesson.

It is also where my interest in **curriculum development as scholarship** began: the questions
of how students build practical judgment, and how a course can be structured to develop it,
are the same questions I now want to research.
