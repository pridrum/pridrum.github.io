For nineteen years I have worked on one kind of problem in different settings: how a group of organizations that do not answer to each other decides where to act, when what counts as a better outcome is contested and does not reduce to a single number. I have asked it of a project-based middle school in Brazil, a league of nonprofit soccer clubs, a network of women’s crisis centers across 450 municipalities, a border checkpoint, Department of Labor inspections, and a multi-agency anti-trafficking task force.

Two conditions recur, and they are why my methods look varied. The decision is distributed, so no single organization controls the outcome. And performance is multi-dimensional and measured indirectly, so the measure has to be built before anything can be optimized. I treat building it as part of the research. In Rio Grande do Sul, evaluating the network of services for survivors of domestic and gender-based violence, a single budget figure would have said the system was underfunded. What we found was that it was too narrow: the range of services available constrained effectiveness more than the total amount invested.

The dissertation

I am a PhD candidate in Industrial Engineering at Northeastern, in the Operations Research and Social Justice Lab, working with Prof. Kayse Lee Maass. I defend on 2 December 2026.

Anti-trafficking work in agricultural labor is carried out by several organizations acting on the same operations without knowing what the others are doing. The network interdiction literature almost always assumes either that a single actor intervenes on a given element, or that multiple interventions combine independently. Neither matches how task forces work, and the assumption changes where the budget should go.

My model drops it. Two of the results are aimed at a management audience: the cost of assuming independence, and the coordination gap between a centralized task force and the same organizations spending the same total separately. Together they tell an agency when coordination is worth its cost. The dissertation is four papers, and the model paper is my job market paper.

Teaching

I have taught eight university courses across four universities and more than 300 students, and before that spent three and a half years teaching middle school in Brazil. In Spring 2026 I was instructor of record for a graduate operations research course, and that year I received the College of Engineering Outstanding Graduate Teaching Award.

I cannot know what class to teach until I know who is in the room. So I ask, and then I change the class. The teaching page is the evidence for that, including the parts where it cost me something.

Outside that

I grew up in Rio de Janeiro, taking apart my sister’s toys with my parents’ tools to see whether I could make them do something else. Most of it did not work and I loved all of it. In between then and now I studied fine arts and design at UFRJ, worked in finance, ran a small business, taught middle schoolers to build boats, and moved countries twice.

I paint watercolors, sew, and skate. Endurance sport and art taught me the same thing, which is that discomfort and failure are part of learning, and that progress comes from showing up with purpose.

  3. Job Market Paper
    Collaboration in Anti-Trafficking Efforts: A Network Interdiction Problem with Multiple Dependent Attacks
    Priscila de Azevedo Drummond and Kayse Lee Maass
    2026
    Working paper. Results analysis. Target: \textitProduction and Operations Management, September 2026
    Abs Bib

    Anti-trafficking agencies often intervene on the same network without knowing what the others are doing. The literature almost always assumes either that one actor intervenes on a given element, or that multiple interventions combine independently, and neither matches how task forces work. This paper defines an axiom class of effect functions rather than a single functional form, places independence at the boundary of that class, and gives an exact linearization with dual bounds. It asks two questions aimed at a management audience: what a decision-maker loses by assuming independence, and how large the coordination gap is between a centralized task force and the same agencies spending the same total separately.
